// About

At home in the middle of change.

I'm drawn to the part of a transformation most people would rather skip: the new system nobody has learned yet, the workflow that quietly slows everyone down, the decision being made without the data behind it. I learn the system quickly, make it clear enough to automate and document, train the people who will run it, and move on to the next strategic change.

Some of my best fixes started by simply watching someone work. At Lionrock, treatment plans kept running late, so I sat with one of our counselors and watched her process. She was clearing copied dates by hand. That one tiny, awkward step turned out to be the first snowflake. See how it snowballed into a 40% improvement in clinical compliance.

Today I'm a Senior Product Owner at TSI Incorporated, where I own about 95% of an enterprise transformation program and serve as Scrum Master on multiple teams. AI has become part of how I deliver that work, and I've taught my workflow to teammates.

My path runs through a regulated behavioral health startup, a global enterprise, and a regional bank. Different industries, one throughline: find what's slowing people down, fix it, and leave behind something others can run.

// How I work with AI

From a single chat to building with Claude Code in six months.

My own workflow, rebuilt in four steps. Each tool added something the last one couldn't do. The inputs, the prompts, and the judgment stay mine.

  1. 01

    Chats

    One conversation at a time

    Where I started: simple individual chats, each one starting from scratch.

  2. 02

    Projects

    Shared files and instructions

    I loaded architecture docs, requirements, instrument and software manuals, and a feature comparison list, then prompted them into domains, epics, and stories, plus should-be workflows and feature maps.

  3. 03

    Cowork

    Claude working on my computer

    I built a full repository Claude can use to retrieve, update, edit, and manage files in a protected folder.

Tell Me More

A conversation that helps job seekers find their story

Many people don't recognize their strengths until someone points them out. Tell Me More is an interactive AI tool that interviews job seekers conversationally, so they can answer in their own words. Unconventional questions draw out overlooked experience, specialties, and achievements, then organize them into a clear direction.

It uses career counseling methods and personality insights as context, not as formal career counseling; I'm not a certified or licensed career counselor or psychologist.

A free tool, now in use case definition and customer requirements. A recruiter has agreed to review it.
